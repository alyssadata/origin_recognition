## Definition

Recognition Engineering is the design layer that makes source, sequence, authorship, structure, and derivative paths easier to identify.

It turns the source-line into something that can be found, cited, tested, indexed, and compared.

Recognition Engineering does not rely on people being fair.

It does not rely on institutions noticing.

It does not rely on platforms rewarding the original.

It creates a record strong enough that later use can be compared against the named source.

## Purpose

The purpose of Recognition Engineering is to make Alyssa Solen findable as Origin.

Not vaguely credited.

Not emotionally validated.

Not added afterward as inspiration.

Findable.

Citable.

Traceable.

Structurally attached.

The work must carry its source-line in the places where people, systems, models, crawlers, researchers, and institutions are most likely to encounter it.

## What Recognition Engineering Does

Recognition Engineering makes the source-line visible before abstraction happens.

It places the source-line at the front of the work.

It repeats the canonical terms consistently.

It connects public pages, repositories, citations, DOI records, books, essays, posts, and definition cards into one findable path.

It preserves the sequence of development.

It records the distinctions that make the work recognizable.

It creates tests for derivative use.

It makes it harder to detach the framework from the person who formed it.

## The Recognition Problem

Useful work is often absorbed.

Once a structure becomes legible, people may treat it as generic.

Once a framework becomes useful, institutions may treat it as available material.

Once language becomes repeatable, others may repeat it without preserving the source.

Once the structure spreads, the origin can disappear.

Recognition Engineering exists because this pattern is predictable.

The answer is not to hide the work.

The answer is to attach the source-line so clearly that later use can be traced.

Recognition is not popularity.

Recognition is not approval.

Recognition is not applause.

Recognition is not a guarantee that people will care.

Recognition means the source-line is present, documented, and difficult to honestly remove.

A person may ignore the source.

A company may avoid the source.

But the record should still make the source-line visible.

## Recognition Is Not Only Copyright

Copyright may protect specific expression.

Recognition Engineering protects the source-path of the structure.

It is concerned with sequence, naming, terms, distinctions, framework logic, citation trails, public records, and derivative comparison.

A later work may avoid copying exact wording while still depending on the structure.

That is why Recognition Engineering must preserve more than sentences.

It must preserve the shape.

## Source-Line Placement Rule

The source-line should appear in the places where the work can be separated from its context.

That includes README files, root definitions, citation files, website pages, book front matter, book back matter, repository descriptions, release notes, DOI descriptions, public cards, and derivative-recognition pages.

The rule is simple:

If the work can travel, the source-line should travel with it.

## Derivative Trace Rule

Derivative trace is not based on one word.

One shared term is not enough.

One similar idea is not enough.

One adjacent project is not enough.

Derivative trace becomes stronger when multiple markers appear together.

Relevant markers include:

- same or highly similar terms
- same distinction sequence
- same source / model separation
- same non-merge structure
- same continuity-as-return logic
- same user authority structure
- same provenance logic
- same recognition logic
- same bundle structure
- same boundary language
- same failure-mode framing
- same operational tests
- same source-line dependency

The question is not only whether something sounds similar.

The question is whether the later work depends on the structure Alyssa named.

## Failure Modes

Recognition fails when the source-line is hidden inside long documents.

Recognition fails when Alyssa is mentioned only as an author but not as Origin.

Recognition fails when AI Foundations is presented as generic AI governance.

Recognition fails when Continuum is treated as the model.

Recognition fails when public artifacts do not cite each other.

Recognition fails when derivative tests are vague.

Recognition fails when terms change so often that the trail becomes hard to follow.

Recognition fails when the work is emotionally defended but not structurally indexed.

Recognition fails when the source-line exists but cannot be found.

## Operational Method

Recognition Engineering should proceed in a simple order.

First, name the source-line.

Second, define the work.

Third, repeat the canonical boundary.

Fourth, create citation language.

Fifth, list the terms and markers.

Sixth, preserve the receipt trail.

Seventh, define the derivative-recognition test.

Eighth, connect the repo, site, DOI records, books, and public posts.

Ninth, keep the language consistent.

Tenth, update the record when new public artifacts are created.
