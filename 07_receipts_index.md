## What Counts as a Receipt

A receipt is any traceable record that helps show:

where the work appeared

when the work appeared

what language was used

what terms were defined

what distinctions were made

what structure was published

what source-line was attached

how the work developed

how later work may compare

whether citation language was included

whether the no-derivative boundary was included

A receipt can be small.

A receipt can be large.

A receipt matters when it helps preserve the path.

A receipt supports recognition.

A receipt does not authorize derivative use.

## Receipt ID Format

Use a simple receipt ID format.

Recommended format:

```text
OR-YYYY-MM-DD-001
```

Example:

```text
OR-2026-05-19-001
```

Meaning:

```text
OR = Origin Recognition
YYYY-MM-DD = date of record or artifact
001 = receipt number for that date
```

If the exact date is unknown, use the best available date and note the uncertainty.

## Receipt Discipline

Receipts should be precise.

Do not inflate the meaning of a receipt.

Do not treat one receipt as proof of all claims.

Do not confuse suspicion with evidence.

Do not confuse similarity with derivation.

Do not confuse public visibility with public ownership.

Do not confuse output with provenance.

Do not confuse citation with vague credit.

Do not confuse citation with permission.

Do not confuse acknowledgement with authorization.

Do not confuse derivative work with Alyssa Solen’s personal work.

Do not confuse a screenshot with a full record when stronger records are available.
